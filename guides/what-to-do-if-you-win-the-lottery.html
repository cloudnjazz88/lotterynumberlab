What Should You Do If You Win the Lottery?

A large lottery win creates several decisions at once, but it does not require an immediate public announcement or a rushed claim. Verify the result, protect the ticket and follow the selling jurisdiction's rules.

Published September 28, 2026 · Checked against official Powerball, Mega Millions, IRS, FTC, and state-lottery claim guidance

A large lottery win creates several decisions at once, but it does not require an immediate public announcement or a rushed trip to lottery headquarters. First verify the result, protect the ticket, identify the lottery that sold it and learn that jurisdiction's claim rules.

The right process depends on the prize amount and where the ticket was purchased. A $20 ticket and a jackpot are not claimed the same way. An individual ticket and a ticket bought by a group can also raise different ownership questions.

This checklist explains the order in which to handle those questions. It is general information, not legal, tax or financial advice.

The short version

  1. Verify the result through an official lottery source.
  2. Protect the original ticket and preserve a clear record of it.
  3. Do not publish the ticket or barcode.
  4. Identify the selling jurisdiction and its claim deadline.
  5. Confirm what that jurisdiction requires before signing or completing the ticket.
  6. Document any shared ownership before someone submits the claim.
  7. For a major prize, review disclosure, payout and tax questions before claiming.
  8. Submit the claim only through the selling lottery's official process.

The order matters. Some choices can be reviewed before a claim is filed but become difficult or impossible to change afterward.

1. Verify the ticket against an official result

Start with the official lottery website or an authorized ticket-checking method. Do not rely solely on a social post, news graphic, search snippet or message from another person. Ticket Match can compare a line with published drawings. It is not official claim verification.

Confirm all of the following:

  • the game
  • the drawing date
  • every number on the same play line
  • the separate Mega Ball or Powerball
  • any multiplier printed on the ticket
  • whether the ticket contains more than one play

For Powerball and Mega Millions, the five white balls can match in any order. The bonus ball is separate and must match the bonus ball drawn. Numbers from different play lines or different tickets cannot be combined.

Keep the original ticket even if an app or retailer scan reports a win. The physical ticket, registered online play or other official purchase record is what the selling lottery validates.

2. Protect the ticket and its identifying information

Treat an apparent winning ticket as an important original document.

Keep it dry, flat and away from heat, direct sunlight and anything that could damage the barcode or printed information. Make clear photographs or scans of the front and back for your records, but do not post them online or send them casually.

A photograph is useful documentation. It is not normally a substitute for the original ticket.

Do not expose:

  • the barcode
  • validation numbers
  • a claim number
  • a complete online account screen
  • personal information written on the ticket

If the ticket was purchased through an official lottery account, secure that account. Use a unique password, enable any available account security and preserve the purchase confirmation.

3. Check the selling lottery's instructions before completing the ticket

Many lotteries tell players to sign the back of a winning ticket. For example, Arizona Lottery's official claim instructions begin with signing the ticket.

That does not make one instruction universal for every possible jackpot situation. Tickets, claim forms and ownership rules differ by jurisdiction. A large prize may also involve a group purchase, a trust or another potential claimant structure.

Before writing on a major winning ticket, read the current instructions from the lottery that sold it. If ownership or claim-structure questions exist, obtain jurisdiction-specific advice promptly. Never alter, erase or overwrite information already placed on the ticket.

For an ordinary individual ticket with clear official instructions, follow those instructions exactly.

4. Find the claim deadline and the correct claim location

Powerball states that prizes must be claimed in the jurisdiction where the winning ticket was purchased. Mega Millions likewise directs players to the selling lottery for the applicable deadline and claim procedure.

Claim periods are not uniform. Depending on the jurisdiction and game, a deadline may be measured in days or up to a year from the drawing. Some jurisdictions also give jackpot winners a different period from other draw-game winners.

Write down:

  • the drawing date
  • the exact claim deadline
  • the location or method required for that prize amount
  • whether an appointment is required
  • the identification and tax documents requested
  • any separate deadline for choosing cash or annuity

Do not use an approximate deadline from a national article. Confirm it on the official website of the lottery that sold the ticket. What happens after a deadline passes is covered in what happens to unclaimed lottery prizes.

Small prizes may be payable by a retailer. Larger prizes may require a claim center or lottery headquarters. A multistate game does not mean that any participating state can pay the ticket.

5. Establish who owns the ticket

If you bought the ticket only for yourself, ownership may be straightforward. If coworkers, relatives or friends contributed money, determine the ownership record before anyone claims it.

Preserve relevant evidence such as:

  • a written pool agreement
  • contribution records
  • group messages
  • copies of the ticket shared before the drawing
  • the agreed division of prizes
  • the name of the person holding the original

Do not assume that the lottery will reconstruct an informal agreement after a dispute begins. Do not let one participant claim a group-owned ticket as an individual without first confirming the correct procedure.

A lottery pool is different from multiple independent jackpot-winning tickets. One pool-owned ticket has one prize claim that may then be divided among its owners. Multiple winning tickets divide the jackpot at the game level.

6. Check the disclosure rule before making the claim public

Winner privacy is controlled mainly by the jurisdiction where the ticket was purchased and claimed. There is no single national anonymity rule. Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction rules are in Can Lottery Winners Stay Anonymous?

Depending on the jurisdiction, a winner's name may be:

  • protected broadly
  • protected only above a prize threshold
  • withheld temporarily
  • generally subject to public disclosure
  • handled under a rule that distinguishes name, city, prize and other claim information

A trust or LLC is not automatically the same as anonymity. Some lotteries permit entity claims, some disclose the entity, some require information about the people behind it and some do not allow a trust to claim the ticket.

Check the selling jurisdiction's current disclosure rule before assuming that a legal entity, declining a photograph or avoiding an interview will keep a winner's identity confidential.

7. Build a small professional team for a major prize

A routine prize does not require a team of advisers. A life-changing prize can justify independent legal, tax and financial advice before the claim is submitted.

Look for professionals whose duties are clear and whose compensation you understand:

  • an attorney licensed in the relevant jurisdiction
  • a tax professional familiar with large gambling winnings
  • a financial adviser whose registration, services and fees can be independently verified

The Pennsylvania Lottery's winner materials encourage jackpot and top-prize winners to seek trustworthy financial and legal advice. The purpose is not to create a complicated structure for its own sake. It is to understand decisions that may become permanent after the claim.

Be cautious when an adviser approaches you first, promises secrecy without reviewing the applicable law or pressures you to transfer money quickly.

8. Separate three decisions that are often confused

Claiming the ticket

This establishes the valid winner under the selling lottery's procedure.

Choosing cash or annuity

A jackpot winner may have to choose between the available cash value and the advertised annuity. This decision concerns timing and form of payment, not whether the ticket won.

Planning for taxes and the money afterward

Federal and possibly state or local tax rules affect what the winner ultimately keeps. Federal withholding is not necessarily the final federal tax bill.

Do not choose cash or annuity solely by comparing the advertised jackpot with the cash value. The decision can depend on taxes, spending control, investment assumptions, estate planning and personal circumstances.

Lottery Number Lab's cash-versus-annuity and tax guide explains the mechanics separately. This checklist is about preparing for that decision, not selecting one option for every winner.

9. Expect tax documentation

The IRS treats lottery prizes as gambling winnings. Certain payments produce Form W-2G, which reports the winnings and any federal income tax withheld.

Keep copies of:

  • the validated ticket or official purchase record
  • the claim form
  • the payment election
  • Form W-2G
  • withholding records
  • documents showing group ownership or divided winnings
  • professional invoices and written advice relevant to the claim

Withholding is a prepayment, not a guarantee that the entire federal obligation has been satisfied. State and local treatment depends on the relevant jurisdictions.

10. Use only official claim channels

A genuine ticket should be handled through the lottery that sold it. Be skeptical of calls, emails or messages claiming that an unrelated organization must release the prize.

The Federal Trade Commission warns that a demand to pay an advance fee for “taxes,” processing, shipping or customs before receiving a supposed prize is a sign of a scam.

A real lottery prize may create actual tax obligations, but those obligations do not begin with sending gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers or banking credentials to an unsolicited caller.

If you did not buy or otherwise officially enter the game being described, a message saying that you won is not evidence of a prize.

What not to do

  • Do not post a ticket photo or barcode.
  • Do not combine numbers from different play lines.
  • Do not assume every jurisdiction has the same deadline.
  • Do not assume a trust or LLC guarantees anonymity.
  • Do not let one pool member claim the ticket without resolving ownership.
  • Do not rush the cash-or-annuity election.
  • Do not treat federal withholding as the final tax calculation.
  • Do not pay an unsolicited person to “release” a prize.
  • Do not miss a real claim deadline while trying to create the perfect plan.

Practical takeaway

Verify first, protect the original and then follow the rules of the jurisdiction that sold the ticket. A major winner usually has more than one issue to resolve, but those issues should remain separate: ownership, deadline, disclosure, payout choice and taxes.

Move carefully, but do not confuse care with delay. Confirm the real deadline immediately and work backward from it. If lottery play stops feeling optional, responsible play resources are available.

FAQ

What is the first thing to do after winning the lottery?
Verify the ticket through an official source and secure the original. Then check the selling lottery's instructions, claim deadline and disclosure rules before making a major claim public.
Should I sign a winning lottery ticket immediately?
Many lotteries instruct individual winners to sign the ticket, but procedures differ. Follow the official instructions of the lottery that sold it. For a major prize involving a group, trust or ownership question, obtain jurisdiction-specific guidance before completing or altering the ticket.
Can I claim a Powerball or Mega Millions ticket in another state?
No. Powerball and Mega Millions prizes must be claimed through the jurisdiction where the ticket was purchased.
Should I tell anyone before claiming?
Keep the circle limited while you verify the ticket and rules. If the ticket is group-owned, communicate with the owners and preserve the ownership record. For a large prize, qualified professional advice may be appropriate before public disclosure or claiming.
Do lottery taxes come out automatically?
Certain winnings are subject to federal reporting and withholding, but withholding may not equal the final tax due. State and local rules may also apply.
Can a trust claim the prize and keep me anonymous?
Not automatically. Trust and entity-claim rules vary, and a trust is not the same as statutory anonymity. Check the selling jurisdiction's rules before relying on that approach.
How long do I have to claim?
The deadline depends on the jurisdiction, game and sometimes prize type. Confirm the exact deadline with the lottery that sold the ticket.
Does a lottery charge a fee before releasing a prize?
Official claim and tax procedures may apply, but an unsolicited demand to send money, gift cards, cryptocurrency or banking details to release a supposed prize is a major scam warning.
