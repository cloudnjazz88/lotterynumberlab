How to read these tables

Each row is one drawing: the five white balls in ascending order, then the Mega Ball or Powerball highlighted at the end. The order the balls came out of the machine does not matter for prizes, so results are always published sorted.

Sum is the total of the five white balls. It clusters near 174 because there are far more combinations that add up to a middling total than to an extreme one — the reason is explained in what real winning combinations look like. Odd/even and low/high count how many of the five balls fell on each side of that split, with "low" meaning 1–35 for Mega Millions and 1–34 for Powerball.

The archive starts at each game's most recent matrix change rather than at its launch, because older drawings used different ball pools and cannot be pooled with current ones without distorting every frequency. That story is worth reading in full.
